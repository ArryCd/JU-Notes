Thermo Laws & Carnot
Laws at a glance

0th Law: If A=B and B=C in thermal equil., then A=C. Defines temperature.

1st Law: $dU = \delta Q - \delta W$ (conservation of energy). $U$ is a state function, $Q$ and $W$ are path functions.

2nd Law (Kelvin-Planck): No engine operating in a cycle can convert all heat from a single reservoir into work.

2nd Law (Clausius): Heat cannot spontaneously flow from cold to hot without external work.

3rd Law: As $T\to 0$, $S\to 0$ for a perfect crystal.

Carnot Engine — key results

Carnot cycle: two isothermals + two adiabatics.

$\eta_\text{Carnot} = 1 - \dfrac{T_2}{T_1} = \dfrac{W}{Q_1} = \dfrac{Q_1 - Q_2}{Q_1}$ $T_1$ = source, $T_2$ = sink. Max efficiency for any engine operating between these temps.
$\text{COP (refrigerator)} = \dfrac{Q_2}{W} = \dfrac{T_2}{T_1 - T_2}$ For Carnot refrigerator: $W = Q_1 - Q_2 = Q_2(T_1-T_2)/T_2$

From $Q_1/T_1 = Q_2/T_2$ (reversible cycle): $\sum_i Q_i/T_i = 0$ and $\oint dS = 0$.

PYQ Worked: Two reversible engines in series
Engine 1 takes heat from source $T_1$, rejects to sink at 770 K. Engine 2 uses 770 K as source, rejects to 250 K. Both do same work. Find $T_1$.
Strategy: Equal work output means equal efficiency for both engines.

Engine 1: $\eta_1 = 1 - 770/T_1 = W/Q_1$
Engine 2: $\eta_2 = 1 - 250/770 = 520/770$

Equal work means: heat absorbed by engine 2 = heat rejected by engine 1 = $Q_{12}$
So: $Q_{12}/Q_1 = 770/T_1$ and work of engine 1: $W_1 = Q_1 - Q_{12} = Q_1(1 - 770/T_1)$
Work of engine 2: $W_2 = Q_{12}(1 - 250/770) = Q_{12}(520/770)$

Setting $W_1 = W_2$: $Q_1(1 - 770/T_1) = (Q_1 \cdot 770/T_1)(520/770)$
$\Rightarrow T_1 - 770 = 520 \Rightarrow$ $T_1 = 1290\text{ K}$
Carnot refrigerator: water at 0°C → ice at 0°C, room at 27°C. Find heat thrown to room, work done, COP.
Given: 1 kg water at 0°C → ice at 0°C. Latent heat of ice $L = 80$ cal/g $= 80000$ cal.
$T_2 = 273$ K (cold), $T_1 = 300$ K (room).

Heat absorbed from cold reservoir: $Q_2 = 80000$ cal
COP $ = T_2/(T_1-T_2) = 273/27 = 10.11$
Work done: $W = Q_2/\text{COP} = 80000/10.11 = 7912$ cal
Heat thrown to room: $Q_1 = Q_2 + W = 80000 + 7912 \approx 87912$ cal

Alternatively: $Q_1 = Q_2 \cdot T_1/T_2 = 80000 \times 300/273 \approx 87912$ cal
PYQ: Equivalence of Kelvin-Planck and Clausius
Show KP ↔ Clausius by contradiction
KP violated → Clausius violated:
Suppose a device D violates KP: absorbs $Q$ from hot reservoir and converts all to work $W=Q$, no heat rejected.
Use this work to run a refrigerator R that takes $Q'$ from cold and delivers $Q'+W$ to hot.
Net effect: $(Q'+W) - Q = Q'$ flows from cold to hot with no other change → Clausius violated. ✓

Clausius violated → KP violated:
Suppose device D violates Clausius: $Q$ flows from cold to hot with no work.
Run a Carnot engine between hot ($T_1$) and cold ($T_2$): takes $Q_1$ from hot, does work $W$, rejects $Q_2 = Q_1 - W$ to cold.
Set $Q_2 = Q$ (the spontaneous flow). Net: hot loses $Q_1 - Q = W$, cold loses and gains $Q_2=Q$ → net result: work $W$ extracted from single reservoir → KP violated. ✓

Entropy — SKS Sir
Properties of Entropy
  • Real physical quantity like $P, V, T$ — but cannot be felt
  • Remains constant in an adiabatic process
  • Differentiates one adiabatic from another — gives rank of adiabatic
  • Single-valued function — depends on state only, not history
  • When entropy increases: system moves from ordered → disordered state (less probable → more probable)
  • Denoted by $S$
Mathematical Definitions
$S = k_B \ln \Omega$ Boltzmann (Statistical)
$dS = \dfrac{\delta Q_\text{rev}}{T}$ Clausius (Thermodynamic)
$\oint \dfrac{dQ}{T} = 0$ (reversible)     $\oint \dfrac{dQ}{T} \leq 0$ (irreversible)

From Carnot: $Q_1/T_1 = Q_2/T_2$ for any reversible cycle → $\sum_i Q_i/T_i = 0$

Entropy of a Perfect Gas PYQ B1

Start from $dQ = dU + PdV = C_v dT + PdV$, divide by $T$, use $P = nRT/V$:

In terms of T and V: $\Delta S = S_2 - S_1 = C_v \ln\dfrac{T_2}{T_1} + nR\ln\dfrac{V_2}{V_1}$
In terms of T and P: $\Delta S = C_p \ln\dfrac{T_2}{T_1} + nR\ln\dfrac{P_1}{P_2}$ $= C_p \ln\dfrac{T_2}{T_1} - nR\ln\dfrac{P_2}{P_1}$
💡 Exam trick
The two expressions are equivalent. For isothermal: $\Delta S = nR\ln(V_2/V_1) = -nR\ln(P_2/P_1)$. For isochoric: $\Delta S = C_v\ln(T_2/T_1)$. For isobaric: $\Delta S = C_p\ln(T_2/T_1)$.

Entropy and Unavailable Energy

$W_\text{available} = Q_1\left(1 - \dfrac{T_R}{T_1}\right)$    where $T_R$ is the lowest available temperature Unavailable energy $= Q_2 = (Q_1/T_1)\cdot T_R \propto$ Entropy change

So: Unavailable energy $\propto$ entropy. As entropy increases, more energy becomes unavailable for doing work.

05
TdS Equations & $C_p - C_v$

Since $S$ is a state function of any two variables, treat it as $S(V,T)$, $S(T,P)$, or $S(P,V)$:

1st TdS Equation — S(V,T): $TdS = C_v\,dT + T\!\left(\dfrac{\partial P}{\partial T}\right)_V\!\!dV$ Use Maxwell: $(\partial P/\partial T)_V = (\partial S/\partial V)_T$
2nd TdS Equation — S(T,P): $TdS = C_p\,dT - T\!\left(\dfrac{\partial V}{\partial T}\right)_P\!\!dP$ Use Maxwell: $-(\partial S/\partial P)_T = (\partial V/\partial T)_P$
3rd TdS Equation — S(P,V): $TdS = C_v\!\left(\dfrac{\partial T}{\partial P}\right)_V\!\!dP + C_p\!\left(\dfrac{\partial T}{\partial V}\right)_P\!\!dV$

Deriving $C_p - C_v$ PYQ B2

Subtract 1st TdS equation from 2nd at constant pressure ($dP=0$):

General result: $C_p - C_v = T\!\left(\dfrac{\partial V}{\partial T}\right)_P\!\!\left(\dfrac{\partial P}{\partial T}\right)_V$
Perfect gas: $PV = RT$ (per mole)
$\left(\dfrac{\partial P}{\partial T}\right)_V = \dfrac{R}{V}$, $\left(\dfrac{\partial V}{\partial T}\right)_P = \dfrac{R}{P}$
$C_p - C_v = T \cdot \dfrac{R}{P} \cdot \dfrac{R}{V} = \dfrac{R^2 T}{PV} = R$

Since $PV = RT$, so $C_p - C_v = R$ ✓

Real gas (vdW): $\left(P+\dfrac{a}{v^2}\right)(v-b) = RT$
$\left(\dfrac{\partial P}{\partial T}\right)_V = \dfrac{R}{v-b}$, $\left(\dfrac{\partial V}{\partial T}\right)_P \approx \dfrac{v-b}{T} + \dfrac{2a}{RT^2}$
$C_p - C_v = R\!\left(1 + \dfrac{2a}{vRT}\right)$

Since $v-b \approx v$ and $a/v^2$ term matters.

Variation of $C_v$ with Volume & $C_p$ with Pressure

From 1st TdS — since S is perfect differential: $\left(\dfrac{\partial C_v}{\partial V}\right)_T = T\!\left(\dfrac{\partial^2 P}{\partial T^2}\right)_V$ Perfect gas: $\partial^2 P/\partial T^2 = 0$ → $C_v$ independent of V. Real gas: $\partial^2 P/\partial T^2 = 0$ too → $C_v$ independent of V for vdW gas also!
From 2nd TdS — since S is perfect differential: $\left(\dfrac{\partial C_p}{\partial P}\right)_T = -T\!\left(\dfrac{\partial^2 V}{\partial T^2}\right)_P$ Perfect gas: $\partial^2 V/\partial T^2 = 0$ → $C_p$ independent of P. Real gas: $(\partial C_p/\partial P)_T = +2a/RT^2$ → $C_p$ varies with P.

J-T Effect & Clausius-Clapeyron
Joule-Thomson (Adiabatic Throttling)

Gas forced through porous plug adiabatically. Work done: $W = P_1V_1 - P_2V_2$. Since $dQ=0$:

$U_1 + P_1V_1 = U_2 + P_2V_2$ → $H = \text{const}$ → isenthalpic process

$\mu_{JT} = \left(\dfrac{\partial T}{\partial P}\right)_H = \dfrac{1}{C_p}\!\left[T\!\left(\dfrac{\partial V}{\partial T}\right)_P - V\right]$ J-T coefficient (from 2nd TdS eq. with dH=0)
  • $\mu_{JT} > 0$ → cooling on expansion
  • $\mu_{JT} < 0$ → heating on expansion
  • $\mu_{JT} = 0$ at inversion temperature $T_i$
J-T for van der Waals gas PYQ B3

From vdW equation, $({\partial V}/{\partial T})_P = R/[P + a/v^2 - 2a/v^2]$

$\mu_{JT} = \dfrac{1}{C_p}\!\left(\dfrac{2a}{RT} - b\right)$ J-T coefficient for vdW gas
$T_i = \dfrac{2a}{bR}$    (Inversion temperature)
Cooling condition: $T < T_i$ → $\mu_{JT} > 0$ → gas cools
Heating condition: $T > T_i$ → $\mu_{JT} < 0$ → gas heats
H₂ and He: $T_i$ very low, so at room temp they heat up!

Internal Energy Variation with Volume

$\left(\dfrac{\partial U}{\partial V}\right)_T = T\!\left(\dfrac{\partial P}{\partial T}\right)_V - P$ From dU = TdS - PdV and 1st Maxwell relation
Perfect gas: $(\partial P/\partial T)_V = R/V$ → $(\partial U/\partial V)_T = P - P = 0$ ✓ (Joule's law)
Real gas: $(\partial U/\partial V)_T = a/v^2$ → internal energy increases as volume increases (intermolecular attraction)

Clausius-Clapeyron Equation (1st Order Phase Transitions)

At phase equilibrium: $g_l = g_v$ (Gibbs potential equal). From $dg_l = dg_v$:

$\dfrac{dP}{dT} = \dfrac{S_g - S_l}{V_g - V_l} = \dfrac{L}{T(V_g - V_l)}$ Clausius-Clapeyron equation. $L = T(S_g - S_l)$ = latent heat.
Applications
Boiling (vaporisation): $V_g \gg V_l$ → $dP/dT = LP/RT^2 > 0$ always.
Melting of ice: $V_\text{liquid} < V_\text{ice}$ → $dP/dT < 0$ → melting point decreases with pressure.

Statistical Mechanics — Ensembles
Microcanonical Ensemble

Isolated system

Fixed: $E, V, N$

All accessible microstates equally probable.

$S = k_B \ln \Omega(E,V,N)$

Temperature defined by: $\dfrac{1}{T} = \left(\dfrac{\partial S}{\partial E}\right)_{V,N}$

Canonical Ensemble

System in heat bath

Fixed: $T, V, N$. Exchange: Energy

Probability: $P_i = e^{-\beta E_i}/Z$

$Z = \sum_i e^{-\beta E_i}$    $\beta = \dfrac{1}{k_B T}$
$F = -k_B T \ln Z$
Grand Canonical Ensemble

Open system

Fixed: $T, V, \mu$. Exchange: Energy + Particles

Probability: $P_{i,N} = e^{-\beta(E_i - \mu N)}/\Xi$

$\Xi = \sum_{N=0}^{\infty} e^{\beta\mu N} Z_N$
$\Omega = -k_B T \ln \Xi$ (grand potential)
QuantityMicrocanonicalCanonicalGrand Canonical
Key function$\Omega(E,V,N)$$Z(T,V,N)$$\Xi(T,V,\mu)$
Entropy$S = k_B\ln\Omega$$S = -(\partial F/\partial T)_V$$S = -(\partial\Omega/\partial T)_{V,\mu}$
Free energy—$F = -k_BT\ln Z$$\Omega = -k_BT\ln\Xi$
Avg. energy$E$ fixed$\langle E\rangle = -\partial(\ln Z)/\partial\beta$$\langle E\rangle = -(\partial\ln\Xi/\partial\beta)_{\mu,V} + \mu\langle N\rangle$
Pressure$P = T(\partial S/\partial V)_E$$P = -(\partial F/\partial V)_T$$P = -(\partial\Omega/\partial V)_{T,\mu}$
⚠ Key distinction
For N distinguishable particles: $Z_N = (Z_1)^N$ (no 1/N! factor — e.g. solid, fixed lattice sites, HO systems).
For N indistinguishable particles: $Z_N = (Z_1)^N / N!$ (e.g. ideal gas — Gibbs correction factor).

Classical Ideal Gas

Hamiltonian: $H = \mathbf{p}^2/2m$. In canonical ensemble with $N$ indistinguishable particles:

Single-particle partition function:
$Z_1 = \dfrac{1}{h^3}\int d^3q\,d^3p\, e^{-\beta p^2/2m} = \dfrac{V}{h^3}(2\pi mk_BT)^{3/2} = \dfrac{V}{\lambda^3}$
Thermal de Broglie wavelength:
$\lambda = \dfrac{h}{\sqrt{2\pi mk_BT}}$    (thermal de Broglie wavelength) Condition for classical ideal gas: $V/N \gg \lambda^3$ (particles far apart)
N-particle partition function: $Z_N = \dfrac{1}{N!}\left(\dfrac{V}{\lambda^3}\right)^N$
Using Stirling: $\ln Z_N = N\ln(V/\lambda^3) - N\ln N + N = N\ln(V/N\lambda^3) + N$

Law of Atmospheres (canonical ensemble application)

Ideal gas in uniform gravitational field $\mathbf{g} = -g\hat{z}$. Potential: $U(z) = mgz$.

$n(z) = n_0\, e^{-mgz/k_BT}$     $P(z) = P_0\, e^{-mgz/k_BT}$ Barometric formula. $n_0$ = number density at $z=0$.

Classical Harmonic Oscillator Systems
⚠ Critical distinction for exam
Harmonic oscillators are on fixed lattice sites → they are DISTINGUISHABLE → $Z_N = (Z_1)^N$, no 1/N! factor. Gibbs correction does NOT apply.

1D Classical HO — N Distinguishable PYQ C2

$H = \dfrac{p^2}{2m} + \dfrac{1}{2}m\omega^2 x^2$. Both momentum and position integrals are Gaussian:

Momentum integral: $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\beta p^2/2m}dp = \sqrt{2\pi m/\beta} = \sqrt{2\pi mk_BT}$
Position integral: $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\beta m\omega^2 x^2/2}dx = \sqrt{2\pi/(m\omega^2\beta)} = \sqrt{2\pi k_BT/(m\omega^2)}$
Single-particle partition function: $Z_1 = \dfrac{1}{h}\sqrt{2\pi mk_BT}\cdot\sqrt{\dfrac{2\pi k_BT}{m\omega^2}} = \dfrac{k_BT}{\hbar\omega}$
Quantity1D HO
$Z_N$$(k_BT/\hbar\omega)^N$
$F$$-Nk_BT\ln(k_BT/\hbar\omega)$
$\langle E\rangle$$Nk_BT$
$C_v$$Nk_B$
$S$$Nk_B[1 + \ln(k_BT/\hbar\omega)]$

2D Classical HO — N Distinguishable In notes

$H = \dfrac{p_x^2+p_y^2}{2m} + \dfrac{1}{2}m\omega^2(x^2+y^2)$ — 4 degrees of freedom per particle:

$Z_1^{(2D)} = \left(\dfrac{k_BT}{\hbar\omega}\right)^2$     $Z_N = \left(\dfrac{k_BT}{\hbar\omega}\right)^{2N}$
$\langle E\rangle = 2Nk_BT$    $C_v = 2Nk_B$    $F = -2Nk_BT\ln\!\left(\dfrac{k_BT}{\hbar\omega}\right)$
$S = 2Nk_B\!\left[1 + \ln\!\left(\dfrac{k_BT}{\hbar\omega}\right)\right]$
💡 Equipartition Theorem
Each quadratic degree of freedom contributes $\frac{1}{2}k_BT$ to average energy.

1D HO: 2 d.o.f. (1 KE + 1 PE) → $\langle\varepsilon\rangle = k_BT$
2D HO: 4 d.o.f. → $\langle\varepsilon\rangle = 2k_BT$
3D HO: 6 d.o.f. → $\langle\varepsilon\rangle = 3k_BT$ (Dulong-Petit: $C_v = 3Nk_B$)

Microcanonical — N 1D Classical HO PYQ C2

Find phase space volume $\Omega(E,V,N)$ for $H = \sum_{i=1}^N \left(\dfrac{p_i^2}{2m} + \dfrac{1}{2}m\omega^2 q_i^2\right) = E$.

The surface $H = E$ is a $2N$-dimensional ellipsoid with semi-axes $a_i = \sqrt{2mE}$ (momentum) and $b_i = \sqrt{2E/m\omega^2}$ (position).

$\Omega(E) \propto \dfrac{1}{N!}\left(\dfrac{E}{\hbar\omega}\right)^N$ Phase space volume (shell between E-ΔE and E)
$S(E,V,N) = k_B\left[N\ln\!\left(\dfrac{E}{N\hbar\omega}\right) + N\right]$ Using Stirling. This gives $E = Nk_BT$ consistent with canonical result ✓

Grand Canonical Ensemble
PYQ CO4 Q4 — Grand canonical probability & partition function
Open system S can exchange heat and particles with large reservoir. Find $P_{i,N_S}$ and grand canonical partition function $\Xi$.
Setup: System S in microstate $i$ with energy $E_i$ and particle number $N_S$. Reservoir has $E_R \gg E_S$, $N_R \gg N_S$.

Probability $\propto$ number of reservoir states when system is in $(i, N_S)$:
$P_{i,N_S} \propto \Omega_R(E_R - E_i, N_R - N_S) = e^{S_R(E_R - E_i, N_R - N_S)/k_B}$

Expand $S_R$ using $\partial S_R/\partial E_R = 1/T$ and $\partial S_R/\partial N_R = -\mu/T$:
$S_R \approx S_R(E_R, N_R) - E_i/T + \mu N_S/T$

So: $P_{i,N_S} \propto e^{-\beta(E_i - \mu N_S)}$

Normalising with grand canonical partition function:
$\boxed{P_{i,N_S} = \dfrac{e^{-\beta(E_i - \mu N_S)}}{\Xi}}$     $\boxed{\Xi = \sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\sum_i e^{-\beta(E_i - \mu N)} = \sum_{N=0}^{\infty} e^{\beta\mu N} Z_N}$
where $Z_N$ is the canonical partition function for $N$ particles. $\mu$ is the chemical potential.

Grand Canonical: Classical Ideal Gas

$Z_N = \dfrac{1}{N!}\left(\dfrac{V}{\lambda^3}\right)^N$ → $\Xi = \sum_{N=0}^\infty \dfrac{1}{N!}\left(\dfrac{Ve^{\mu/k_BT}}{\lambda^3}\right)^N = \exp\!\left(\dfrac{V}{\lambda^3}e^{\mu/k_BT}\right)$
Grand potential: $\Omega = -k_BT\ln\Xi = -k_BT \cdot \dfrac{V}{\lambda^3}e^{\mu/k_BT}$
QuantityGrand Canonical Result
Average particle number$\langle N\rangle = \dfrac{1}{\beta}\dfrac{\partial\ln\Xi}{\partial\mu} = k_BT\cdot\dfrac{\partial\ln\Xi}{\partial\mu} = \dfrac{V}{\lambda^3}e^{\mu/k_BT}$
Chemical potential$\mu = k_BT\ln\!\left(\dfrac{\langle N\rangle\lambda^3}{V}\right)$ (from inverting above)
Average energy$\langle E\rangle = -\dfrac{\partial\ln\Xi}{\partial\beta}\bigg|_{\mu,V} + \mu\langle N\rangle = \dfrac{3}{2}\langle N\rangle k_BT$

Relativistic Ideal Gas

Ultrarelativistic Gas ($E = pc$) PYQ C3

Hamiltonian: $H = \sum_{i=1}^N |\mathbf{p}_i|c = \sum_i p_i c$. Canonical partition function for $N$ indistinguishable particles:

Single-particle momentum integral using spherical coords in momentum space:
$Z_1^\text{mom} = \int d^3p\,e^{-\beta pc} = 4\pi\int_0^\infty p^2 e^{-\beta pc}\,dp$
Substitute $u = \beta pc$, $p = u/\beta c$, $dp = du/\beta c$:
$= 4\pi\left(\dfrac{1}{\beta c}\right)^3\int_0^\infty u^2 e^{-u}\,du = \dfrac{4\pi}{(\beta c)^3}\cdot\Gamma(3) = \dfrac{4\pi\cdot 2}{(\beta c)^3} = \dfrac{8\pi}{\beta^3 c^3} = 8\pi(k_BT)^3/c^3$
Position integral gives $V$, so:
$Z_1 = \dfrac{V}{h^3}\cdot\dfrac{8\pi(k_BT)^3}{c^3} = \dfrac{8\pi V(k_BT)^3}{h^3 c^3} = 8\pi V\!\left(\dfrac{k_BT}{hc}\right)^3$
$N$-particle: $Z_N = \dfrac{1}{N!}(Z_1)^N = \dfrac{1}{N!}\left[\dfrac{8\pi V(k_BT)^3}{h^3c^3}\right]^N$
QuantityUltrarelativistic result
$\ln Z_N$$-\ln N! + N\ln\!\left[\dfrac{8\pi V(k_BT)^3}{h^3c^3}\right] \approx N\ln\!\left[\dfrac{8\pi V(k_BT)^3}{Nh^3c^3}\right] + N$
Free energy $F$$F = -Nk_BT\!\left\{1 + \ln\!\left[\dfrac{8\pi V(k_BT)^3}{Nh^3c^3}\right]\right\}$
Internal energy$U = -\dfrac{\partial\ln Z}{\partial\beta} = 3Nk_BT$

⚠ Compare with non-relativistic
Non-rel: $U = \frac{3}{2}Nk_BT$. Ultrarel: $U = 3Nk_BT$ (twice as much!). Non-rel: $Z_1 \propto T^{3/2}$. Ultrarel: $Z_1 \propto T^3$.

General Relativistic Gas ($E = \sqrt{p^2c^2 + m^2c^4}$)

$Z_1 = \dfrac{4\pi V m^2 c^2}{h^3 \beta} K_2(\beta mc^2) = \dfrac{4\pi V m^2 c k_BT}{h^3} K_2\!\left(\dfrac{mc^2}{k_BT}\right)$ Limiting cases: $m\to 0$ or $T\to\infty$: $K_2(\alpha)\to 2/\alpha^2$ → recovers ultrarelativistic result

Black Body Radiation & Planck
PYQ CO4 Q5 — Planck's hypothesis → distribution → Wien's law
State Planck's quantum hypothesis. Derive Planck's distribution. Show Wien's displacement law from it.
Planck's Quantum Hypothesis:
A harmonic oscillator of frequency $\nu$ can only have discrete energies: $E_n = nh\nu$ where $n = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$ and $h$ is Planck's constant. Energy is exchanged in quanta of $h\nu$.

Mean energy of a quantum oscillator:
Single-oscillator partition function: $Z_1 = \sum_{n=0}^\infty e^{-n\beta h\nu} = \dfrac{1}{1-e^{-\beta h\nu}}$

Mean energy: $\langle\varepsilon\rangle = -\dfrac{\partial\ln Z_1}{\partial\beta} = \dfrac{h\nu}{e^{h\nu/k_BT}-1}$

Planck's energy density:
Number of modes in $[\nu, \nu+d\nu]$: $g(\nu)d\nu = \dfrac{8\pi V\nu^2}{c^3}d\nu$ (density of states for EM field)

$u(\nu)d\nu = \dfrac{8\pi h\nu^3/c^3}{e^{h\nu/k_BT}-1}\,d\nu$     (Planck distribution)
Wien's displacement law:
Maximise $u(\nu)$: differentiate, set to zero. Let $x = h\nu/k_BT$:
$\dfrac{d}{d\nu}[u(\nu)] = 0$ → $3(1-e^{-x}) = x e^{-x}$ → solved numerically: $x \approx 2.82$
So $h\nu_\text{max}/k_BT \approx 2.82$ → $\nu_\text{max} \propto T$ → $\lambda_\text{max}T = b = \dfrac{hc}{2.82k_B} \approx 2.898 \times 10^{-3}$ m·K

Alternatively in $\lambda$ form: $u(\lambda) \propto \lambda^{-5}/(e^{hc/\lambda k_BT}-1)$, maximum at $\lambda_\text{max}T = \text{const}$.
Limiting cases of Planck's law

Wien's law (high freq / low T: $h\nu \gg k_BT$)

$e^{h\nu/k_BT} \gg 1$, so $e^{h\nu/k_BT}-1 \approx e^{h\nu/k_BT}$:

$u(\nu) \approx \dfrac{8\pi h\nu^3}{c^3}e^{-h\nu/k_BT}$

Rayleigh-Jeans (low freq / high T: $h\nu \ll k_BT$)

$e^{h\nu/k_BT}-1 \approx h\nu/k_BT$:

$u(\nu) \approx \dfrac{8\pi\nu^2}{c^3}k_BT$   (classical, "ultraviolet catastrophe" at high $\nu$)
Key results to memorise
$u(\nu) = \dfrac{8\pi h\nu^3/c^3}{e^{h\nu/k_BT}-1}$   (Planck distribution)
$\lambda_\text{max} T = 2.898\times10^{-3}$ m·K   (Wien's displacement)
$U_\text{total} = \sigma T^4 V$   where $\sigma = \dfrac{2\pi^5 k_B^4}{15h^3c^3}$ (Stefan-Boltzmann)

Quantum oscillator partition function: $Z = 1/(1-e^{-\beta h\nu})$

Mean energy: $\langle E\rangle = h\nu/(e^{h\nu/k_BT}-1)$

Probability & Statistics
PYQ CO3 Q1 — Gaussian distribution, mean, most probable, SD
$f(x) = Ae^{-(x-5)^2/2}$, $-\infty < x < \infty$. Find normalisation $A$, mean, most probable value, and standard deviation.
Comparison with standard Gaussian: $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-(x-\mu_0)^2/2\sigma^2}$

From $f(x) = Ae^{-(x-5)^2/2}$: $\mu_0 = 5$ (centre), $2\sigma^2 = 2$ → $\sigma = 1$

Normalisation: $\int_{-\infty}^\infty Ae^{-(x-5)^2/2}dx = A\sqrt{2\pi} = 1$ → $A = 1/\sqrt{2\pi} \approx 0.399$

Mean value: $\langle x\rangle = \dfrac{\int_{-\infty}^\infty x\, f(x)\,dx}{\int f\,dx} =$ $5$ (by symmetry, since $f$ is symmetric about $x=5$)

Most probable value: Maximum of $f$ → $df/dx = 0$ → $x-5 = 0$ → $x_\text{mp} = 5$

Standard deviation: $\sigma^2 = \langle(x-\langle x\rangle)^2\rangle = \int(x-5)^2 f(x)\,dx$
Using $\int u^2 e^{-u^2/2\sigma^2}du = \sigma^2\sqrt{2\pi}$: $\sigma^2 = 1$ → $\sigma = 1$

Summary: Mean = Most probable = 5, SD = 1. For Gaussian, all three coincide.

💡 Last-minute memory anchors
  • β = 1/k_BT — everything involves β. High T: β small → high energy states accessible.
  • Ideal gas: U = (3/2)Nk_BT. Ultrarel: U = 3Nk_BT. HO(1D): U = Nk_BT.
  • Distinguishable (HO lattice): no 1/N!   Indistinguishable (gas): divide by N!
  • J-T: H = const (isenthalpic). Cooling: $T < T_i = 2a/bR$.
  • TdS Eq. 1: $T$, $V$ independent. TdS Eq. 2: $T$, $P$ independent.
  • Planck mean energy: $h\nu/(e^{h\nu/k_BT}-1)$ — denominator is the key. At low T: → 0 (quantum freeze-out). At high T: → $k_BT$ (classical limit).
  • For PYQ C Q2: phase space of $N$ HOs → $2N$-dim ellipsoid. $\Omega\propto E^N$. $S = Nk_B\ln(E/N\hbar\omega) + \text{const}$ → $E = Nk_BT$.
